# J-pipe-Trompe

Hydro-Passive Rural Energy & Cold Chain Infrastructure for Off-Grid Mountain Communities

Target Sector and Global Development Goals • SDG 1: No Poverty • SDG 2: Zero Hunger • SDG 7: Affordable and Clean Energy • SDG 13: Climate Action

The Problem: Rural Energy and Food Loss In remote mountain communities, especially in low-income agricultural regions, post-harvest losses are severe. Up to 30–40% of fruits and vegetables are lost before reaching the market because communities lack affordable and reliable cold-chain infrastructure.
Traditional cold-storage systems are often not viable in these areas because they depend on: • Grid electricity or diesel generators • Expensive mechanical refrigeration systems • Frequent maintenance and technical support • Vulnerability to power outages and voltage instability

The result is a recurring cycle of low productivity, food waste, reduced incomes, and weak rural economic resilience.

The Solution: This project is a hydro-passive infrastructure concept designed to transform the natural energy of mountain streams into multiple essential rural services without electricity, fuel, or moving machinery.
This system uses a dual-loop hydraulic arrangement in which water flowing down a mountain slope creates natural pressure differentials and compressed air conditions. The system is designed to convert the available hydraulic potential into: • Cold storage • Vacuum-based dehydration • Compressed air energy for local processing and village use

In practical terms, the concept creates a decentralized rural utility hub that provides:

Refrigeration: +2°C to +4°C
Vacuum conditions: -300 to -500 mbar
Compressed air power: +6 bar (if you need more air pressure or air volume, just change few variables ex:pipe diameter, height difference for input/output water levels)
This approach is intended to provide essential agricultural and village infrastructure without dependence on fossil fuels or grid electricity.

Technical Concept and Geographic Flexibility The system is designed to be deployed directly along natural mountain terrain using standard HDPE or PVC piping, without the need for deep drilling or costly vertical shaft construction.
The design aims to adapt to local topography using a universal slope-based model: L = 180 / sin X

For example: • On a 30° mountain slope • A 240-meter inlet pipe and 120-meter J-pipe exit loop may be used to capture the required hydrostatic counterweight • This can generate and stabilize continuous pressure of approximately +6 bar

This makes the concept potentially adaptable to varied mountainous regions worldwide, particularly where natural water flow and elevation differences are available.

Why This Matters This concept is designed around a low-cost, durable, and resilient model for rural infrastructure:
• Low Capital Cost The system relies on standard piping and static components rather than electrical refrigeration or compressor machinery, potentially reducing installation costs by 60% or more compared with conventional systems.

• Zero Operating Cost The system does not require electricity or fuel once installed. It leverages natural gravity and stream flow as its ongoing energy source.

• Minimal Maintenance There are no pistons, compressors, lubricants, or rotating mechanical systems. The primary maintenance task is routine clearing of debris from the intake mesh.

• Rapid Return on Investment Because the system removes electricity bills, generator fuel costs, and repair expenses, it may amortize within 1.5 to 3 years depending on the site and usage model.

• Cleaner and Safer Rural Infrastructure The process is designed to naturally filter air through water contact during descent, helping remove dust and particulates before air is introduced into storage and processing systems.

Intended Impact The proposed system is aimed at creating a self-sustaining infrastructure model for mountainous farming communities by: • Reducing food spoilage • Extending shelf life of fruits and vegetables • Supporting rural income generation • Encouraging local food processing and value addition • Eliminating dependence on diesel and unstable grids • Supporting climate-resilient, off-grid agricultural development
Closing Statement HIPEK-SZM is designed as a climate-smart, low-cost, and decentralized infrastructure solution for rural mountain communities facing the dual challenge of energy access and food loss. By combining passive hydraulic pressure, cold-chain preservation, vacuum dehydration, and compressed air utility generation, the project seeks to create a resilient model for sustainable rural development.
